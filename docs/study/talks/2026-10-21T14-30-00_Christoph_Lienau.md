## 主讲人简介
Prof. Christoph Lienau holds the Chair Professorship of Experimental Physics at Carl von Ossietzky Universität Oldenburg. He is a leading pioneer in ultrafast nano-optics. He obtained his Ph.D. in Physical Chemistry from the University of Göttingen, followed by postdoctoral research in Prof. Ahmed H. Zewail’s group at Caltech. Afterwards, he worked at the Max-Born-Institute for Nonlinear Optics and Short Pulse Spectroscopy, and has been a full professor at Oldenburg University since 2006. His research interests cover electron dynamics on femtosecond and nanometer scales, including two-dimensional electronic spectroscopy (2DES), near-field optics, exciton-plasmon strong coupling, strong-field nano-optics and ultrafast electron dynamics. He has published over 250 publications with more than 15000 citations, including high-impact papers in Science, Nature Nanotechnology, Nature Photonics, Nature Chemistry, PRL and Reviews of Modern Physics. He is an Optica Fellow, and has served as program committee member for multiple international conferences and vice chair of the Semiconductor Physics Division of the German Physical Society.

## 讲座简介
Strong coupling plays a key role in modern solid state physics. It can occur in diverse systems such as layered atomically thin semiconductors, microcavities, polariton condensates and high temperature superconductors. Coherent coupling among electrons, excitons, phonons and photons generates hybrid quasiparticles with distinct properties compared with uncoupled constituents. In this talk, I will present recent progress exploiting tunable open Fabry-Pérot cavities to overcome such limitations. I will report 10-fs-resolution 2DES experiments on J-aggregate emitters inside open FP cavities. The measurements reveal multimode Rabi oscillations persisting for hundreds of femtoseconds at room temperature, far longer than the bare exciton dephasing time. The combination of 2DES and tunable open-cavity spectroscopy offers a promising platform to investigate multimode Rabi oscillations and Floquet physics in nonlinear polaritonic systems with long room temperature coherence.
