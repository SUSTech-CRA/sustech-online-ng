## 讲座简介
Directional maximal operators average a function over long, thin rectangles whose orientations are drawn from a prescribed set of directions. Their analytic behaviour depends in subtle ways on the geometric and combinatorial structure of that set: sufficiently sparse, or lacunary, families yield bounded operators, while richer direction sets can generate Kakeya-type phenomena and dramatically different estimates.In this talk, I will describe a tree-based framework for measuring the complexity of a set of directions. The central invariant, called the splitting number, records the extent of persistent branching in the associated direction tree. I will explain its connection with classical notions of finite-order lacunarity and show how this perspective leads naturally to estimates for directional maximal operators. The resulting picture brings together harmonic analysis, geometric measure theory, and combinatorics, and provides a useful language for distinguishing genuinely lacunary direction sets from those capable of supporting Kakeya-type constructions.This is joint work with Edward Kroc and Juyoung Lee.
