## 主讲人简介
Xiaodong Zou is a full professor at the Department of Chemistry, Stockholm University. She received her Ph.D. in structural chemistry from Stockholm University in 1995 and became a full professor in 2005. Zou's research focuses on the development of electron crystallographic methods and new porous materials. She is a pioneer in developing electron crystallography for structure determination of polycrystalline materials. Her group has developed methods that enable atomic structure determination from crystals too small for conventional single-crystal X-ray diffraction and applied them to a broad range of materials, including zeolites, metal-organic frameworks, covalent organic frameworks, pharmaceuticals, and proteins. Zou is a Distinguished Professor of the Swedish Research Council, Wallenberg Scholar, Fellow of the Royal Society of Chemistry, Fellow of The World Academy of Sciences (TWAS), and Honorary Fellow of the Chinese Chemical Society. She received the IVA Gold Medal in 2024 and is an elected member of both the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences and the Royal Swedish Academy of Engineering Sciences. She is also member of the Nobel Committee for Chemistry.

## 讲座简介
Approaching the Nobel Prize

The Nobel Prize is widely regarded as one of the highest honors for scientific achievements and contributions to humanity, reflecting Alfred Nobel's vision of recognizing discoveries that bring the greatest benefit to humankind. This lecture will begin with a brief introduction to Alfred Nobel's life and the history of the Nobel Prize, followed by an overview of the nomination and selection processes. It also offers insights into the Nobel Prize ceremonies and celebrations.

Throughout its history, the Nobel Prize has recognized groundbreaking discoveries that have transformed our understanding of nature and shaped the development of modern science and technology. From X-ray diffraction and quantum mechanics to electron microscopy, advanced materials, and artificial intelligence, these discoveries illustrate how advances in physics, chemistry, and other disciplines are interconnected, opening new opportunities for scientific exploration and innovation.

Drawing on my nearly 40 years of research experience, I will share how Nobel Prize-winning discoveries have inspired and shaped my own scientific career. In particular, I will reflect on my journey in developing electron crystallography methods for determining the atomic structures of materials and molecules. Through personal experiences, I hope to illustrate how scientific curiosity, persistence, interdisciplinary collaboration, and the desire to understand the unknown can lead to new discoveries and inspire the next generation of scientists.
